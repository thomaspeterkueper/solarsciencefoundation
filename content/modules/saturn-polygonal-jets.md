# Saturn Hexagon: Why can a jet become polygonal?

Status: learning-path seed

## Learning outcome
Learners can distinguish an observed atmospheric structure from laboratory analogues and competing dynamical explanations.

## Path
1. Rotation and Coriolis effects
2. Zonal and circumpolar jets
3. Shear and barotropic instability
4. Planetary/Rossby-wave interpretations
5. Rotating-fluid laboratory analogues
6. Stratification and thin-shell approximations
7. Internal heat forcing
8. Stability and persistence
9. Model discrimination: what observation would distinguish mechanisms?

## Interactive demonstrator contract
Inputs: rotation rate, shear/jet speed, stratification proxy, heat-forcing proxy, perturbation amplitude/wavenumber.
Outputs: qualitative flow regime, dominant wave/polygon mode where supported by the selected model, stability indicator, and explicit model/evidence badge.

Never present the control panel as a literal Saturn simulator unless calibrated against a cited physical model.

## Exercise
Given an observed six-fold pattern and a laboratory experiment producing a hexagon, classify which statements are observation, analogue, model inference, and unresolved hypothesis.

## NOXIA transfer
Completion teaches atmospheric-model comparison. It must not automatically unlock a claim that Saturn's hexagon has been 'solved'.

Sources: NASA Cassini; Aguiar et al. 2010; Espa & Lacorata 2026; Johnson 2026; exact Lagrangian perturbation study 2026.
